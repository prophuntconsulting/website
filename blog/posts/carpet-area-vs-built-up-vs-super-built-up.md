Two flats advertised at "1,200 sq ft" can have very different amounts of usable space. The difference comes down to which area is being quoted. This guide explains carpet area vs built-up area vs super built-up area, what RERA changed, and how to compare prices fairly.

## The three areas, in plain terms

| Term | What it includes | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Carpet area** | The usable floor inside your flat, including internal walls | The legal basis for selling under RERA |
| **Built-up area** | Carpet area plus the thickness of the external walls, plus balconies in many older quotes | Older brochures, some resale listings |
| **Super built-up area** | Built-up area plus your share of common areas: lobby, lifts, staircases, clubhouse, corridors | Older brochures; not accepted for pricing under RERA |

The further down the table you go, the bigger the number, and the less of it you can actually walk on.

## What RERA says about carpet area

The [Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/real-estate/real-estate-regulation-development-act-2016/pdf/) defines carpet area in section 2(k) as:

> "the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by the external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment."

In practice that means:

- **Included:** the floor of every room, kitchen and toilet, plus the area under the internal walls between them
- **Excluded:** external walls, service shafts, and any balcony, verandah or open terrace that belongs only to your flat

Balconies and terraces are not hidden. Under RERA disclosures and the Maharashtra model agreement for sale, they are listed **separately** from the carpet area, so you can see exactly what you are getting.

## Why this changed how flats are sold

Before RERA, flats were often priced per sq ft of super built-up area. Because the "loading" for common areas varied from project to project, two prices per sq ft could not be compared.

Since RERA, the carpet area of each flat has to be disclosed, and in Maharashtra the [MahaRERA model agreement](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in) prices the flat on its carpet area, with balconies and terraces shown separately. When you read an agreement, the carpet area is the number that matters.

## How big is the "loading"?

Loading is the extra area added on top of carpet area. It varies widely, but as a rough guide:

- **Built-up area** is typically about 10% to 15% more than carpet area
- **Super built-up area** is often 25% to 40% more than carpet area, depending on how much common space the project has

These are typical ranges, not rules. A project with a large clubhouse and wide corridors will have a higher loading than a simple building.

## A worked example

Say a brochure quotes a flat at **1,200 sq ft super built-up** for **₹96 lakh**, which is **₹8,000 per sq ft** on super built-up area. The real question is what you pay per sq ft of floor you can use.

"30% loading" can mean two different things, which changes the answer:

| If 30% loading means… | Carpet area | Price per sq ft of carpet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Super built-up = carpet + 30% of carpet | about 923 sq ft | about ₹10,400 |
| Common areas are 30% of super built-up | 840 sq ft | about ₹11,430 |

Either way, the effective rate is well above the ₹8,000 on the brochure. Always ask for the **RERA carpet area** of the exact unit, and compare projects on price per sq ft of carpet area.

## If the final carpet area changes

Flats are measured again after construction. The Maharashtra model agreement for sale allows for a small variation and spells out what happens if the final carpet area differs from the agreed figure:

- if the carpet area is **less** than agreed, the promoter must refund the excess amount you paid, with interest, within a set period
- if it is **more**, the promoter can ask for payment for the extra area, but only within a cap of about 3% and at the agreed rate

Check the exact clause in your own agreement before you sign. If it differs from the model agreement, the change should be highlighted.

## What to check before you book

- Ask for the **RERA carpet area** of the exact unit, not the tower average.
- Check the **balcony and terrace areas** separately, and whether they are open or enclosed.
- Make sure the carpet area in the **allotment letter, agreement and MahaRERA registration** match.
- Compare projects on **total cost per sq ft of carpet area**, including parking and other charges.
- Visit a **sample flat** or a built unit and measure a room or two if you can.
